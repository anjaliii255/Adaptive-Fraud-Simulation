## The base rate, and why it breaks comparability

At **0.12991%** this anchor sits **31x below** the synthetic default's
measured **4.03%**.

That is more than an order of magnitude, so the two are not comparable and no operating point carries across. A 1% FPR here buys 13,232 false positives against 1,719 fraud rows, so precision@k collapses in a way it never does on synthetic traffic. Thresholds must be set at this rate, and synthetic numbers never share a table with these.

Train and test sit at comparable base rates.

## Data integrity

Counted from the file, not assumed. The loader clamps a non-positive amount to 0.01 and logs the
count rather than dropping the row, because dropping rows silently moves the base rate.

| check | rows | share |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| amount <= 0 | 19 | 0.001436% |
| self-transfer (src == dst) | 181 | 0.013679% |

**181 self-transfers are present in real traffic.** `afl/attack/realism.py` treats a self-transfer as an impossible row and penalises the generator for emitting one. On this anchor that rule is a modelling choice, not a fact about payments — ticket 14 derives its bounds from here and has to decide which.

## Quirks

- Synthetic (multi-agent AML simulator): a stand-in for real AML graphs, not real data.
- Only two typologies present (fan_in 783, cycle 936), not the seven AMLSim can generate.
- TX_TYPE is TRANSFER on all 1,323,234 rows. The column carries no information and is not read.
- IS_FRAUD is a real boolean in transactions.csv and the lowercase strings true/false in accounts.csv. The loader normalises through str() so either parses.
- alerts.csv has 1,719 rows but only 391 distinct ALERT_ID values; TX_ID is the unique key.
- Every fraud row carries an ALERT_ID (none are -1), so the typology join loses nothing.
- 19 rows carry TX_AMOUNT == 0, which the contract rejects. The loader clamps them to 0.01 and logs the count rather than dropping them.
- 181 rows are self-transfers (sender == receiver). afl/attack/realism.py penalises the generator for emitting one, so on this anchor that rule is a modelling choice rather than a fact about payments. Ticket 14 has to decide which.

## What it cannot tell us

- Nothing about card or UPI behavioural fraud; that is PaySim's job and the injected vectors'.
- No device, no merchant, no geo beyond a country code on the account.
- Base rate 0.130% matches PaySim, and both sit ~130x below the synthetic default.
