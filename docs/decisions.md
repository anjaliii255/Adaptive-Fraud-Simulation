# Graded decisions and reason codes

_Generated by `scripts/build_decisions.py` from the anchors on disk. Every number below traces to
`artifacts/decisions/<anchor>.json`, which carries the cost model, the calibration, the split
digest and the seed that produced it. Do not edit this file — re-run `make decisions`._

`make baseline` says how well the detector **ranks**. This says what happens when a rank becomes
an action somebody has to work, and the two are different questions.

## The bands are derived, and there is nowhere left to type one

Before this, `calibrate_to_fpr` put `decline_at` at the target FPR and the other three bands at
0.8, 0.6 and 0.3 of it. Those ratios were calibrated to nothing. On the PaySim M3 fold they
landed friction on 45.6% of holdout traffic while precision@100 was 0.00 — blanket friction,
reported in the same table as a detection metric.

Now every action is priced at the transaction's own probability and its own amount, and the one
that minimises expected cost is the one taken. `config/defend/lgbm.yaml` has no band numbers in
it at all. The four `*_at` values still exist because a model card and an auditor need a
threshold to read, but they are computed from the cost model and
`tests/test_decision.py::test_the_derived_ladder_and_the_per_transaction_price_agree` holds the
two readings to the same answer.

## The cost model

`config/costs/default.yaml`. Every number carries a `why` and `CostModel.from_config` **refuses
to load one whose `why` is blank** — a comment can be deleted and nothing notices, a required
field cannot.

Flat costs are quoted as multiples of `unit_amount`, the anchor's median payment, and resolved
to currency at load. That indirection is load-bearing: PaySim's median payment is 74,872 and
AMLSim's is 157, so a flat cost of "4.0" would open the review band at a probability of 0.00005
on one anchor and never open it on the other, while looking equally principled in both artefacts.

| parameter | resolved value | why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `fraud_loss_rate` | 1 | Share of the amount lost when fraud is allowed through. 1.0 — the whole amount. On an instant A2A or UPI rail the funds are irrevocable and there is nothing to claw back, which is the property C3 exists to exploit. Card carries some interchange recovery, but the loss-share is scheme- and case-dependent and recovering it takes months, so 1.0 is the reading that does not flatter the detector. A lower value here makes every band wider. |
| `false_decline_cost` | 0.35 | Cost of wrongly declining a good payment, as a share of its amount. A false decline costs the margin on the payment, the support contact, and a slice of the customer's future volume; the industry's own surveys put the lifetime cost at a multiple of the transaction margin. 0.35 is the mid of the range we are prepared to defend. This is the single number that most moves the decline band, so `make decisions` reports the action mix at 0.20 and 0.50 as well — the sensitivity is measured, not asserted. |
| `step_up_cost` | amlsim 0.6268 · paysim 299.2 | Flat cost of an OTP or in-app challenge, as a multiple of the anchor's median payment (~300 on PaySim, ~0.6 on AMLSim). It prices roughly fifteen seconds of customer friction plus the small share of good payments that are abandoned at the challenge. Flat rather than amount-scaled because abandonment is a behaviour, not a percentage of value. |
| `step_up_efficacy` | 0.8 | Share of fraud a challenge stops. High against a fraudster who does not hold the second factor, which is most of S2 and S3. It stops NOTHING in the two families that matter most here — C2 (the victim is socially engineered into authorising it) and M3 (the account owner IS the fraudster) — so 0.80 is a population average across the taxonomy, not a promise about any one vector. It is set below the rungs above it because a challenge cannot be the top of the ladder. |
| `hold_cost` | amlsim 1.881 · paysim 897.7 | Flat cost of delaying settlement pending an automated recheck — 3x a challenge. On an instant rail a hold is the product promise being broken rather than a moment of friction, so it costs materially more than an OTP and materially less than an analyst's time. |
| `hold_efficacy` | 0.85 | Share of fraud a hold stops. Above a challenge because it buys the minutes the pass-through families trade against — C3's whole design is to move funds onward before anyone looks — but below a review, because a hold with nobody watching it eventually releases. |
| `review_cost` | amlsim 7.835 · paysim 3,741 | Flat cost of putting a case in the analyst queue, as a multiple of the median payment (~3,700 on PaySim, ~8 on AMLSim). About three minutes of a fully loaded analyst, plus the delay to the customer. This is the number that sets how big the queue is, and `precision@100` is what says whether the queue was worth staffing. |
| `review_efficacy` | 0.95 | Share of fraud a human review stops. The best rung short of a decline, and deliberately under 1.0: an analyst working from the same features the model had is not infallible, and a review that stopped everything would dominate the decline outright and remove the top of the ladder. Analysts also let some fraud through on purpose, to keep a case alive. |

## What this is not

**It is not a claim that these are the right costs.** They are defensible numbers with stated
reasoning, and the sensitivity table above is there so a fraud lead can see what changes if they
disagree. The contribution is that the argument is now about eight business numbers with
rationales instead of four thresholds with none.

**It is not measured on the leave-one-attack-out fold.** These are each anchor's own labelled
fraud at the committed boundary. The M3 fold's positives are injected synthetic rows — see the
ticket 07 carry-out and `docs/detector.md` — so an action mix measured there would be partly a
statement about the generator.

**AMLSim's numbers are not evidence about detection.** Every alerted row in that file is a
sub-20 amount against traffic reaching 21.5M, so it is separable before any model runs. Read
PaySim.

`amlworld` is not downloaded, so it has no decision artefact here. `banksim` is not downloaded, so it has no decision artefact here.
