# The anomaly layer on the held-out family

_Generated by `scripts/build_anomaly.py` from the anchors on disk. Every number below traces to `artifacts/anomaly/<anchor>.json`, which carries the split digest, the seed, the model cards and the commensurability audit that produced it. Do not edit this file — re-run `make anomaly`._

Ticket 10. The supervised detector is trained without one attack family and then asked about it; the anomaly layer is fitted on legit traffic alone and asked the same question. The comparison is the deliverable, whichever way it falls.

## The result

**The premise did not hold on any anchor measured.** This layer was built on two claims: that the supervised model collapses on a family it has no labels for, and that an outlier score fitted on legit traffic degrades more gracefully. Measured on amlsim, paysim, neither survives. The supervised model does not collapse. On paysim it scores *higher* on the held-out family than on the anchor's own labelled fraud, which is a finding about the injected rows rather than about the detector. The anomaly layer sits far below it rather than under it. The blend is where the layer earns its keep instead: on paysim the weight curve has an interior optimum, so the two halves together beat either alone.

**Read the two audit lines before each table.** Every positive in these folds is an injected synthetic row and every negative is a real one, so a fold where one contract field separates the two is measuring which generator wrote the row rather than whether anything detects fraud. And a fold with a handful of positives moves several points of recall per row.

## amlsim

**The supervised model neither collapses nor improves on M3.** PR-AUC 1.000 on the held-out family against 1.000 on this anchor's own labelled fraud in the same test window (`artifacts/detector/amlsim.json`) — inside the 0.01 either way that this file treats as a difference.

Read that reference number with the caveat `docs/detector.md` attaches to it: a supervised PR-AUC of 1.000 on amlsim is a property of the generator that wrote the anchor, not evidence about production fraud. Everything compared against it here inherits the caveat.

**The anomaly layer is not the floor it was designed to be.** PR-AUC 0.003 against the supervised model's 1.000 — beaten 295x on the one fold that exists to favour it. The promotion this ticket held open is not earned on this anchor.

**And it loses to the amount floor** — 0.034 for sorting by amount (smallest amount first, chosen on the training window) against the layer's 0.003. A detector that a sort beats has not found anything.

**The blend does not earn its row on this anchor.** The curve rises monotonically to w=1.0 — the supervised model alone, PR-AUC 1.000 — and the shipped w=0.7 costs 0.013 of it (0.987).

**Fitting on the fraud rows too scores higher, not lower** — PR-AUC 0.005 against the legit-only 0.003, with 60.7% of the held-out family allowed through against 52.0%. The legit-only rule is not paying for itself on this fold, and it is kept anyway: the training fraud here is other *known* families, which is not the case the rule exists for.

- **Commensurability** — worst single contract field `log_amount` at PR-AUC 0.0027 against a base rate of 0.0002: ok.
- **Fold size** — 385,515 holdout rows carrying 150 M3 positives, against 930,744 training rows with 1,449 fraud and not one M3 row. Split digest `0400e45485d9de69`.

### The score → probability map

The anomaly layer's *model* sees no labels. Its cost map does, and has to — a cost model compares `p x amount` against a flat analyst cost, so an uncalibrated score makes the arithmetic right and the inputs meaningless. Fitted on the training window's validation tail (179,218 rows, 281 positives), the same tail every other detector uses, never the holdout. Expected calibration error 0.42962 → 0.00049. It is not cosmetic: an isolation forest's raw score sits in [0.358459, 0.685208], so a cost band placed at a genuine probability catches every row until the map has been fitted.

## paysim

**The supervised model does not collapse on M3 — it does *better* there than on this anchor's own fraud.** PR-AUC 0.524 and recall@FPR 1.000 on the held-out family, against 0.152 / 0.478 on the real labelled fraud in the same test window (`artifacts/detector/paysim.json`). That is a finding about the injected rows rather than about generalisation: a family the detector has never seen should not be easier than one it trains on, and this fold flatters whatever is measured on it.

**The anomaly layer is not the floor it was designed to be.** PR-AUC 0.033 against the supervised model's 0.524 — beaten 16x on the one fold that exists to favour it. The promotion this ticket held open is not earned on this anchor.

It does clear the amount floor (0.033 against 0.003, largest amount first), which is the least a trained layer owes.

**The blend does earn its row here**, and the curve has an interior optimum to prove it: w=0.5 reaches PR-AUC 0.551 against 0.524 for the supervised model alone and 0.033 for the anomaly layer alone. The shipped w=0.7 takes 0.017 of the 0.027 available — reported, not adopted, because a weight chosen on the fold it is quoted from is the tuning-on-test the baseline forbids.
