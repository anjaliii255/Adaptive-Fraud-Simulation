00 / THESIS

Three pillars, one loop

Identify, generate and defend are not three deliverables — they are one feedback loop. The attacks we generate become the defender's training set; the defender's blind spots become the attacker's next brief. The single claim the whole system exists to test:

Adaptive adversarial simulation improves detection recall on a held-out attack family versus real-only training and non-adaptive synthetic augmentation — and we can show exactly why.

Red edges = the attacker adapting. Blue edge = the defender retraining on logged evasions. Gold = the honesty check: does it generalise to a family held out of training entirely.

01 / IDENTIFY

Nine vectors, and why each is here

Breadth is a judged criterion, so we carry nine. But nine configurations collapse onto three engines — graph, velocity, drift — so the build cost stays near three. Only the strong three carry the adaptive machinery; the rest are templates or held-out tests.

Strong · run the full loop held-out evaluation lives here
S1Graph engine

Mule network & layering

Fan-out → consolidation, layering chains, rapid pass-through, distributed multi-device mule clusters.

WhyThe richest structure we have: network behaviour reveals fraud even when every single transaction looks legitimate. Perfect substrate for graph features and the adaptive loop — and directly relevant to India's mule-account crackdown.
S2Velocity engine

Card testing / BIN enumeration

Automated validation of generated card numbers: high velocity → low-and-slow → randomised intervals → distributed across cards and merchants.

WhyAdaptation is visually legible — a judge watches the attacker literally walk around a static threshold. Trivial to evaluate, and the cleanest live demonstration of the loop working.
S3Drift engine

Account takeover via drift

Post-compromise behavioural change: new device, new beneficiary, changed velocity — sudden (easy) versus gradual drift (hard).

WhyThe sudden-vs-gradual axis is what makes a sequence model earn its seat instead of decorating the deck. Covers the account-compromise surface both expert reviews flagged as missing.
Common · must-catch load templates in training + fixed benchmarks
C1Drift engine

Bust-out

Long clean tenure, then a sudden utilisation spike across correlated accounts before cash-out.

WhyA dominant real-loss category and a pure sequence problem. Shares the drift engine with ATO, so near-zero marginal build for a whole extra family.
C2Velocity + payee

UPI collect-request / APP scam

Social-engineered push payment: first-time payee, atypical amount, rapid drain, elderly-account profile.

WhyThe dominant India account-to-account scam class. First-time-payee and rapid-drain features are cheap and high-signal, and it keeps the submission on-rail for a Mastercard / GFF audience.
C3Graph + velocity

Instant-A2A pass-through

New payee plus immediate onward drain, cross-institution velocity on rails with no chargeback.

WhyExploits the exact irrevocability property that makes real-time payments hard to defend. Reuses graph and velocity primitives already built for S1 and S2.
Mid · emerging / novelty held-out or free-by-construction
M1Is the mechanism

Boundary probing / paced evasion

Activity deliberately clustered just under known limits; the attacker maps the model's approve/decline boundary and walks along the safe side.

WhyIt costs nothing — it is the adaptive optimiser's search strategy. It is also the cleanest "attack against your own model," which upgrades the taxonomy's rigor.
M2Drift → bust-out

Synthetic-identity lifecycle

Identity fabrication → new-account onboarding → seasoning → payment/credit abuse.

WhyCovers the onboarding surface and models fraud that begins before the first transaction. Built as a template that hands off into the bust-out engine rather than needing its own.
M3Anomaly / drift

First-party / friendly fraud

Legitimate owner abusing the product: chargeback abuse, intentional default, refund abuse.

WhyThe single biggest omission a domain judge will probe. User == fraudster breaks the legit-vs-attacker assumption and is the perfect held-out stress test for the anomaly layer.

Taxonomy hygiene: we present three levels, never flattened — fraud mechanisms (S1, S3, C1, C3), fraud enablers (C2, M2), and attacks against our own AI (M1). Surface coverage: identity/onboarding · account compromise · payment manipulation · network/behavioural · model-level, plus first-party.

02 / GENERATE

The attack system

Structure comes from actors and graphs; local realism comes from a generative model used sparingly; adaptivity comes from search, not reinforcement learning. The LLM proposes scenarios inside a schema — it never emits transactions.

Actors & two-tier generation

Actors
Normal user · fraudster · mule · merchant — each a parameter bundle, not an autonomous agent.Params: amount dist, frequency, inter-arrival, counterparty diversity, account age, shared devices, fan-in/out, cash-out speed.
Tier 1
Deterministic / probabilistic templates for all nine vectors — cheap, reproducible, form the baseline training and fixed benchmarks.
Tier 2
Adaptive optimiser wraps only S1–S3. This is the novel part.

Adaptive optimiser — not RL

Search
Bayesian optimisation (Optuna) or evolutionary search (GA / CMA-ES). Easier to build, debug and explain than RL, and better for a short sprint.
Fitness
evasion_success − λ · realism_penalty
Realism
Schema validation, empirical distribution bounds, graph-validity rules. The attacker never gets unconstrained freedom — that is the answer to "how do you stop unrealistic attacks?"
03 / PROVE IT

Fidelity, measured before the generator

The biggest risk is synthetic data that looks right column-by-column and is useless for training. So realism is defined explicitly across three levels — and utility, not appearance, is the bar.

Level1Statistical

Local statistical fidelity

Do feature distributions and dependencies survive?

KS · Wasserstein · correlation-matrix delta · mutual-information delta
Level2Structural

Behavioural & structural fidelity

Do velocity, inter-arrival, amount transitions and graph motifs — fan-in, fan-out, cycles, path length — reproduce? More important here than Level 1.

motif frequency · velocity match · α-precision / β-recall
Level3Utility

Functional utility — the bar that counts

Does training on it improve detection on a held-out attack family and on future time periods? A beautiful KS score that does not lift recall is a failure.

TSTR gap · held-out recall @ fixed FPR · leave-one-attack-out lift
04 / DEFEND

A stack where every layer earns its seat

Each attack family leaves a different trace: mule rings are a graph problem, bust-out is a sequence problem, card testing is a velocity problem, and a held-out family is, by definition, not a supervised problem at all.

01

LightGBM baseline

Over velocity windows, RFM features, time-since-last-event and graph-derived features. The hard baseline to beat on tabular fraud and the fastest thing to explain.
Workhorse
02

Anomaly layer

Isolation forest / autoencoder. The only thing with a chance on a leave-one-attack-out family with no labels — promote it to headline if supervised recall collapses.
Zero-day
03

Sequence model

GRU or small transformer over per-entity history, self-supervised on unlabelled history first. Only for the drift arc — ATO and bust-out. If it doesn't lift, that is a reported result.
Earn it
04

Temporal GNN

Graph attention over the account-device-beneficiary graph. Baseline is graph-features + LightGBM; the GNN is kept only if it shows statistically meaningful lift.
Experiment
05

Decision & explainability

Graded action — step-up / hold / review / decline — not a binary block, cost-weighted since false declines dominate. SHAP reason codes from day one.
Governance

Evaluation protocol — the part that decides "research vs demo"

Report
PR-AUC · recall @ fixed FPR · precision@k framed as an analyst alert budget.
Never
Accuracy or ROC-AUC alone — at a 0.1–2% base rate both flatter a model that catches nothing.
Split
Out-of-time, never random. Random splits leak the future and inflate every number.
Hero
Leave-one-attack-out. Train without a family, then measure recall on it. This is the number that proves generalisation.
05 / EVIDENCE

The slide that beats a bigger team

Three systems, one evaluation. If our system only improves on attacks it generated itself, the evidence is weak. If it improves on the held-out column, the argument is hard to dismiss.

07 / BUILD

Reproducibility, honesty, ownership

Reproducibility checklist — our real differentiator

  • docker compose up or python run_experiment.py --config reproduces a headline number
  • Global seed set; deterministic where possible
  • Every attack run saves its parameter set and resulting metrics
  • Out-of-time split committed, never regenerated randomly
  • Convergence curve + three-system table regenerate from logs
  • Public repo · pinned deps · README with exact commands
  • Backup recording of the live demo

Honesty guardrails — what moves us 7 → 9

  • C2ST is a diagnostic near chance under our protocol — not proof of realism
  • DCR / MIA are evidence against memorisation — not proof of privacy
  • Synthetic-only reduces exposure — not automatic DPDP compliance
  • Latency budget depends on the decision point — don't assert a fixed sub-50ms fact
  • AI-risk classification depends on deployment context — don't claim a blanket carve-out
  • Projected losses labelled as projections — never as realised figures

TRAIN → DETECT → MEASURE
The shared seam — do this together, first

With two owners the loop only closes where they meet. Agree the schema + metrics contract on day one, then wire an end-to-end skeleton that runs with dummy numbers before either side goes deep. A hollow loop that runs beats two polished halves that never connect.

Cut on purpose — stated as future extension

Multimodal / deepfake detection · LLM social-engineering generator · multiple tabular generators (keep one baseline) · mandatory GNN · full RL · agent-swarm simulation. Naming these as deliberate scope decisions reads as maturity, not gaps.

The story that wins
Not "we built an AI fraud platform." Instead: we tested whether a closed attacker–defender loop makes a detector more robust to attacks it has never seen — and here is the reproducible curve that shows both sides co-adapting.
